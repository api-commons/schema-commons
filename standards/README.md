# Standards

Every standard Schema Commons reads, extracted from the standard's own published source by `scripts/extract-standard.py` (nothing here comes from a provider's spec). A standard serves a context in one of two ways:

- **Measures** -- a census context's core is compared with the standard's objects, and the README shows the coverage both ways (`scripts/standard-pass.py`).
- **Owns the shape** -- a reference context publishes the standard's objects unchanged and measures which providers implement them (`scripts/reference.py`).

## Candidates

Thin contexts whose findings name a standard as the route forward, each one a standards pass waiting to be run:

- **DeFi Protocols** (`defi`): DeFi is widely exposed but not in a common shape: in a 154-provider cohort, liquidity pools (41 readable providers) and swap quotes (36) reach no property at half the providers -- pool address is at 44% and the amount in at 42%. Every aggregator names the sell and buy sides its own way (sell_token, input_mint, src_token, token_in). Protocols agree only on a name. Revisit after a standards pass over the 0x and 1inch quote shapes, which many aggregators copy.
- **Natural Language Processing** (`nlp`): Text analysis is not published in a common resource shape: in a 91-provider cohort, entities at 8 readable providers agree on text and entity_type; sentiment appears at 3. Embeddings are the exception: 24 readable providers agree on model, input, encoding_format and index, which is the OpenAI-compatible interface that api-commons/models already standardizes. They belong there, not in a separate vocabulary.
- **MCP Servers and Agent Registries** (`mcp-servers`): The MCP tag marks the 926 providers that offer an MCP server, not providers that list servers, so a census is the wrong instrument here. Servers at 28 readable providers agree only on name and id (many of those are hosting, game or cloud servers), tools on id, name and description, and agent cards appear at one provider. The shape is set by the protocol and the official MCP registry's server.json, which should be taken in a standards pass, not a market census.
- **Event Streaming and Queues** (`event-streaming`): Messaging infrastructure is not published in a common REST shape: in a 421-provider cohort, topics at 31 readable providers agree only on a name, queues on name and id, and published messages on a name. The consumer-group core (consumer_group_id, kind, partition_assignor) is the Kafka REST v3 resource shape, republished by Kafka-compatible vendors, so it is one standard and not market agreement. The shapes that streaming actually shares live in AsyncAPI and CloudEvents, which a standards pass should take.
- **Kubernetes and Clusters** (`kubernetes`): Managed Kubernetes is not published in a common shape above the Kubernetes API itself: in a 296-provider cohort, clusters at 55 readable providers agree only on a name (id and status 46%, version 36%), and cluster is also the word for database and compute clusters in this cohort. Node pools agree on id, name, status and node_count, but at 7 providers. Workloads and namespaces are the Kubernetes API's own objects, a standard every platform passes through unchanged, so a standards pass over the Cluster API would be a better reference than a census.
- **Container Registries** (`containers`): Container registries share the OCI Distribution API, not a management shape: in a 104-provider cohort, repositories at 15 readable providers agree on name and description; tags at 5 agree on a name; registries at 9 agree on id and name. Pushing and pulling images goes through the OCI distribution protocol, which every registry implements identically, so a standards pass over the OCI specification is the reference here.
- **Threat Intelligence** (`threat-intelligence`): Threat intelligence is not published in a common REST shape: in a 135-provider cohort, indicators at 21 readable providers share no property at half (id reaches 38%), because each lookup returns the vendor's own verdict object for an IP, domain or hash. Threat actors and campaigns agree only on id and name. The field already has its common vocabulary in STIX 2.1 and TAXII, and that is the reference to take in a standards pass.
- **Risk and Compliance** (`risk-management`): Governance, risk and compliance platforms agree on identity and little else: in a 336-provider cohort, risks at 19 readable providers share nothing at half (likelihood, impact and score are each a platform's own scale), controls and frameworks agree on id, name and description, and vendors on id and name. The shared vocabulary of controls and frameworks is NIST OSCAL, which a standards pass should take instead of a census.
- **Asset Tokenization** (`tokenization`): Real-world-asset tokenization is not published in a common shape: in a 113-provider cohort, tokenized assets at 36 readable providers share nothing at half (an asset is a fund at one issuer, a stablecoin at another and a security token at a third), and investors are readable at 3. Transfers agree on amount, status and id, which is the generic transfer already measured in payouts and blockchain-data, not a tokenization concept. The on-chain standards (ERC-3643, ERC-1400) are the reference to take.
- **Agentic Commerce** (`agentic-commerce`): Agentic commerce is new enough that its shape is set by protocols, not by the market: in a 160-provider cohort, checkout sessions at 22 readable providers share nothing at half (id 41%, currency and line_items 32%), and payment mandates appear at 6. The vocabulary that is forming lives in the Agentic Commerce Protocol, the Universal Commerce Protocol and x402. Take those in a standards pass, and re-measure once implementations have shipped.
- **Clinical Trials** (`clinical-trials`): Clinical trials publish one public shape: the ClinicalTrials.gov v2 study record (protocol_section, derived_section, has_results), republished by the 3 providers that wrap it. Studies are readable at 4 providers in a 40-provider cohort, sites at 1 and subjects at none; trial-management (EDC, CTMS) APIs are partner-gated. The reference is the ClinicalTrials.gov data model and CDISC, not a census.
- **Drug Discovery** (`drug-discovery`): Drug discovery publishes few harvested specs: in a 59-provider cohort, compounds are readable at 7 providers (id, name and SMILES each at 43%), targets at 4 and assays at 1. Most companies tagged here are drug developers with no public API. The open chemistry databases (ChEMBL, PubChem) carry the field's shared vocabulary, and their schemas are the reference.
- **Medical Imaging** (`medical-imaging`): Medical imaging publishes almost no harvested OpenAPI: 11 providers, with imaging studies readable at one. Imaging is exchanged through DICOMweb (QIDO-RS, WADO-RS, STOW-RS), a standard every PACS implements, and that is the reference to take.
- **Pharmacy and Prescriptions** (`pharmacy`): Pharmacy is not published openly: in a 45-provider cohort, prescriptions are readable at 3 providers, medications at 3 and pharmacies at 2, with no shared property. E-prescribing runs over NCPDP SCRIPT on the Surescripts network, and drug data comes from NDC, RxNorm and openFDA labels, the references to take.
- **Medical Devices** (`medical-devices`): Medical devices publish almost no harvested OpenAPI: 18 providers, with devices readable at 3 and readings at 1. Device identity is the FDA's GUDID and UDI, and device data travels through IEEE 11073 and FHIR Device and Observation resources, which are the references.
- **Telecommunications** (`telecommunications`): Telecom operators publish one shared shape, and it is a standard rather than market agreement: the network checks at 19 readable providers (number verification, SIM swap, location; phone_number, device_phone_number_verified, max_age) are the GSMA CAMARA APIs, which each operator republishes. Counted once, as a shared standard, nothing else is common: subscribers are readable at 2 providers in a 95-provider cohort, and quality-on-demand sessions at none. CAMARA is the reference; a standards pass should measure it directly.
- **Satellite and Earth Observation** (`satellite`): Earth observation shares its catalog through a standard, not a census: in a 170-provider cohort, scenes at 42 readable providers agree only on name and id, with geometry at 48%, because providers that implement STAC (SpatioTemporal Asset Catalog) and those with their own search APIs shape items differently. Tasking orders agree on id, status and name, but at 7 providers. STAC Item and the OGC API -- Features shape are the reference, and a standards pass should take them.
- **Drones** (`drones`): Drone platforms publish almost no harvested OpenAPI with readable schemas: in a 30-provider cohort, flights are readable at 1 provider and aircraft at none. Airspace and Remote ID data follow the ASTM F3411 and F3548 (UTM) standards, the reference if this is revisited.
- **Maritime and Vessels** (`maritime`): Maritime data is not published in a common shape: in a 228-provider cohort (mostly shipping and logistics companies), vessels at 11 readable providers share no property at half, and positions at 13 reach latitude and longitude at 46%. Ports and port calls are readable at 3 and 4. The shared vocabularies are the AIS message fields and the DCSA (Digital Container Shipping Association) standards for port calls and track-and-trace, which a standards pass should take.
- **Oil and Gas** (`oil-and-gas`): Oil and gas publish almost no harvested OpenAPI: 19 providers, with wells readable at 3 and production volumes at none. Upstream data is exchanged through the Energistics standards (WITSML, PRODML, RESQML) and state regulators' bulk files, which are the references.
- **Mining** (`mining`): Mining publishes almost no harvested OpenAPI: 10 providers with 23 specs, and no readable mine-site or equipment schema. Mining companies are operators, not API providers; their fleet and equipment data sits in vendor telematics (measured in fleet and iot) and in ISO 15143-3 (AEMP 2.0), the reference if this is revisited.
- **Manufacturing** (`manufacturing`): Manufacturing publishes little openly: in an 82-provider cohort, parts at 12 readable providers agree on id, name and part_number, but bills of materials agree only on a name (6 providers). Work orders at 7 carry id, status, dates, location and priority, which is the maintenance work order that property-management already holds, not a production order. Shop-floor data moves over OPC UA and MTConnect, and ISA-95 (B2MML) carries the production-order vocabulary, which are the references.
- **Supply Chain Visibility** (`supply-chain`): Supply-chain visibility adds nothing common beyond what shipping and freight already hold: in a 280-provider cohort, tracked shipments at 31 readable providers agree only on status; suppliers agree on id and name. Milestone events agree on event_type, location and occurred_at, but at 5 providers. Container tracking follows the DCSA Track and Trace standard, and supply-chain events follow GS1 EPCIS, which are the references.
- **Wholesale and B2B Ordering** (`wholesale`): B2B ordering adds no new common shape: in a 151-provider cohort, orders at 29 readable providers agree on id, status and line_items, which is the order commerce and procurement (purchase orders) already hold. Wholesale buyers are readable at 3 providers and price lists at 5. B2B trading runs on EDI (X12 850/855/856, EDIFACT ORDERS), which is the reference vocabulary.
- **Government Services and Permits** (`government`): Government services are not published in a common harvested shape: in a 208-provider cohort (mostly agencies publishing data), service requests are readable at 5 providers and agree only on service_code, the Open311 field. Permits are readable at 1 and inspections at none. Open311 GeoReport v2 is the shared standard for service requests, and the reference.
- **Public Spending and Grants** (`public-spending`): Public spending is published by a handful of government systems, each in its own shape: in a 117-provider cohort, awards are readable at 3 providers and grant opportunities at 5, agreeing only on an id. The shared vocabularies are the Open Contracting Data Standard (OCDS) and the 360Giving and IATI standards, which a standards pass should take.
- **Legislation** (`legislation`): Legislation has one well-shaped public API, and it is a single source: the bill core (session, number, chamber, latest_action, origin_chamber_code) is the Congress.gov API, listed under three catalog names (congress-gov, loc, us-house-of-representatives). Counted once, bills are readable at 4 providers. Legislators at 10 readable providers agree on state, id, name, chamber and party, a real but small agreement, and votes are readable at 3. Akoma Ntoso and the Open States data model are the references for a standards pass.
- **Elections** (`elections`): Elections publish almost no common harvested shape: in a 39-provider cohort, elections are readable at 2 providers, candidates at 4 and contests at 2, with no shared property. Election data is standardized by NIST SP 1500-100 (the Election Results Common Data Format) and the Voting Information Project spec, which are the references.
- **Higher Education** (`higher-education`): Higher education publishes little in a common student-information shape: in a 164-provider cohort (mostly universities), students at 7 readable providers agree only on an id; course sections at 9 agree on id and name; terms agree on a name. Student-information systems keep their APIs institution-gated, and learning management is already measured in lms. The 1EdTech standards (OneRoster, LIS) and Ed-Fi are the references.
- **Course Catalogs** (`course-catalogs`): Course catalogs agree on identity only: in a 91-provider cohort, courses at 26 readable providers agree on id, name and description, and no other property reaches 30% -- not the provider, duration, level, language or URL. That is the course that lms already holds. Course discovery uses schema.org Course and the IEEE LOM and 1EdTech CASE standards, which are the references.
- **Mortgages** (`mortgages`): Mortgages publish little openly: in a 44-provider cohort, loans at 8 readable providers agree on id and loan_amount; borrowers are readable at 2 and rate quotes at 4. Loan-origination systems keep their APIs partner-gated, and the industry's shared vocabulary is the MISMO reference model, the reference to take. Consumer and business lending are already measured in lending.
- **Climate and Emissions** (`climate`): Climate data is not published in a common resource shape: in a 79-provider cohort, air-quality measurements at 9 readable providers share no property at half (the timestamp reaches 44%), climate-risk scores are readable at 1, and monitoring stations agree on name and id. Station observations are the shape weather already holds, and environmental observations are standardized by OGC SensorThings, the reference.
- **Sustainability Reporting** (`sustainability`): Sustainability reporting is not published in a common shape: in a 68-provider cohort, sites at 12 readable providers agree on id and name, consumption records are readable at 3 and metrics at 4 with nothing shared. As with esg, the vocabulary lives in reporting taxonomies (the GRI Standards and the ISSB and ESRS XBRL taxonomies), not in APIs.
- **Robotics** (`robotics`): Robotics publishes almost no harvested OpenAPI: of the companies tagged robotics, 25 have harvested specs, with robots readable at none and missions at 1. Robot fleets are driven through ROS 2 and the VDA 5050 and MassRobotics interoperability standards, which are the references.
- **Autonomous Vehicles** (`autonomous-vehicles`): Autonomous vehicles publish no harvested OpenAPI with these resources: 6 providers with 11 specs, and no trip or drive-log schema. Simulation scenarios are standardized by ASAM OpenSCENARIO and OpenDRIVE, the references if this is revisited.
- **3D Printing** (`3d-printing`): 3D printing publishes little in a common shape: 19 providers, with model files readable at 4 (agreeing on an id), quotes at 1, and print jobs or orders at 8 agreeing on id and status. Model geometry travels as files (STL, 3MF), with 3MF the standard to take.
- **Live Streaming** (`live-streaming`): Live streams are already a concept in video. Measured on their own in a 170-provider cohort, live streams at 38 readable providers agree only on an id; created_at and name reach 47%, so a separate context adds nothing. The stream key and ingest and playback URLs are spelled differently at every platform, and ingest itself is standardized by RTMP, SRT and WHIP, not REST.
- **Online Communities** (`communities`): Online communities publish little in a common shape: in a 68-provider cohort, members at 14 readable providers agree only on an id, discussions at 7 on id and name, spaces are readable at 1 and replies at 3. The post-and-comment shape is the one social-media and knowledge-base already hold, and federated communities use ActivityPub, the reference.
- **Hotels and Property Systems** (`hospitality`): Hotel operations add little beyond travel, which is built from the same Hospitality, Hotels and Vacation Rentals tags. In this 86-provider cohort, hotels at 16 readable providers agree on id, name and address; stays at 12 agree only on id and status (check-in and check-out at 42%) and rate plans only on an id. Hotel distribution runs on the OpenTravel Alliance (OTA) and HTNG messages, which are the references.
